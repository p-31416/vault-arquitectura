## Fuente 2 — ArchDaily, «AD Classics: Los Manantiales / Felix Candela»
URL: https://www.archdaily.com/496202/ad-classics-los-manantiales-felix-candela — snippet

- «Comprised of four intersecting hypars, a strikingly thin roof surface creates a dramatic dining space.»
- «Candela began building thin shell structures not for clients but as full scale experiments… Candela developed forms where stresses could be determined with simple equations. A form Candela called "umbrellas," created by joining four straight edge hypars, were an efficient way to cover large spaces such as markets and warehouses.»
- «The roof is a circular array of four curved-edge hypar saddles that intersect at the center point, resulting in an eight-sided groined vault. The plan is radially symmetric with a maximum diameter of 139 feet. Groins spanning 106 feet between supports.»
- «The largest membrane forces are carried along the intersections between the forms, called the groins. This areas are thickened by creating hidden steel reinforced "V" beams. The rest of the structure has minimal reinforcing… but essentially works entirely in compression… revealing the radical thickness of the 4cm (1 ½") shell.»
- «Narrow boards were used as formwork, following the straight-line generator that forms the hypar surface.»
- «Candela inverted his umbrella form for the footings… Five 1" diameter steel tie-bars link adjacent footings and resist lateral thrust.»
- «The form of Los Manantiales reappears in a later work by Candela in Spain, and has been emulated several times by other designers in locales such as Potsdam and Stuttgart. Candela's work continues to inspire contemporary designers such as Santiago Calatrava.»

- «The formwork for each double-curved shell was entirely created through straight pieces of timber boards, before the rebar was placed and concrete was poured by hand.»

## Fuente 4 — Textbook of Architecture, «Candela: The hyperbolic paraboloid (1958)»
URL: https://textbookofarchitecture.com/source/candela — snippet

- «Candela preferred the hyperbolic paraboloid because its geometry is simple enough to calculate by hand, its double curvature makes a thin shell stiff, and its straight rulings allow the formwork to be made from straight boards.»
- «span ÷ thickness of a good shell ≈ 400–1000 (an egg: ≈ 100)»
- «A thin curved shell carries load by the membrane forces in its surface, not by bending; so shape it to its loads and supports, and it can span great distances with a thickness of a few centimetres.»
- «Umbrellas of four hypars on a single column roofed warehouses and markets; groined vaults of intersecting hypars spanned factories; and the restaurant Los Manantiales at Xochimilco (1958) was covered by eight hypar lobes only about four centimetres thick.»

## Fuente 8 — MIT, Plunkett & Mueller, «Thin Concrete Shells at MIT: Kresge Auditorium and the 1954 Conference»
URL: http://digitalstructures.mit.edu/files/2015-08/plunkett-mueller.pdf — snippet PDF

- Kresge: «a concrete shell on three supports designed by architect Eero Saarinen (1910-1961)» — 1/8 de esfera, luz libre 160' (48,8 m), la mayor de EE.UU.
- «The concrete pour for the shell was completed in April 1954, and the removal of the supporting formwork led to substantial deflections at the free edges, much larger than anticipated. The contractor, George A. Fuller Company, and the engineers, Ammann & Whitney, developed a solution: strengthening nine of the vertical window mullions… to support the gravity load of the shell along its free edges.»
- «The existence of the supporting mullions was not publicly disclosed until September 1954» (Lacy 1954).
- Conferencia MIT junio 1954: Candela, Tedesko, Philip Johnson; «this conference has…cantilevered out from Saarinen's dome».
- Contradicción central de las cáscaras: «on the one hand, they offer an infinite spectrum of possibilities for new structural shapes… On the other hand, their success depends on a precise and sometimes intractable relationship between geometry and structural behavior; just because a shape is rendered in thin concrete does not mean it behaves as an efficient shell.»
- Contexto: formalizadas en Alemania años 20 por Dischinger y Finsterwalder.
