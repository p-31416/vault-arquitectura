## Fuente 1 — Songel Gonzalez, «Tents: a paradigm of lightness and sustainability…», JCHMSD 2022
URL: https://riunet.upv.es/bitstream/handle/10251/190411/...pdf (DOI 10.1108/JCHMSD-05-2021-0097) — snippet PDF

- «Frei Otto's innovative tents and gridshells were not based on form imitation of vernacular architecture, but rather on a thorough understanding of physical form-generating processes, driving specific materials to optimal form, like his experiments with soap film models to generate tensioned minimal surfaces, or his experiments with hanging chain net models to generate compressive antifunicular lattice shells.»
- «Frei Otto (1925-2015)… defined lightweight construction (Leichtbau) as "a way of building with a minimum consumption of material, energetic, and economic means" (Otto, 2010).»
- «he invented and pioneered the use of new types of light structures, such as tensile membranes, cable nets, gridshells or branching structures.»
- Cita de Le Ricolais: «zero weight, infinite span».
- Tipos de tiendas vernaculares analizados: tipi, black tent beduina, yurt.

## Fuente 2 — Boller & D'Acunto, «Structural design via form finding: Comparing Frei Otto, Heinz Isler and Sergio Musmeci»
URL: https://doi.org/10.1201/9781003173434-168 — snippet

- «Form finding is the design process, both physical and digital, that leads to the definition of the form of a structure in static equilibrium under given loads and boundary conditions… a "form-active structure" (Veenendaal & Block 2012) that expresses a "figure of equilibrium" (Linkwitz 1999).»
- «Otto's form finding approach stands as a methodology based on direct observation and the replication of natural processes.»
- Grupo «Biologie und Bauen» fundado 1961.
- Materiales de experimentación de Otto: «sand cones, tensile nets, pneumatic membranes and soap-film models» — «the soap film minimal surface for tensile membranes and the hanging chain catenary form for compressive arch and shell structures. This was the essence of form-finding through physical modeling.» (Goldsmith)

## Fuente 7 — schlaich bergermann partner, «Roof for Munich Olympic Stadium 1972»
URL: https://www.sbp.de/en/project/roof-for-munich-olympic-stadium-1972/ — snippet

- «The roofs are supported by cable net structures consisting of multiple saddle-shaped surfaces framed by edge cables and suspended from masts.»
- «the Munich Olympic roof, virtually the progenitor of all lightweight structures, was designed under the direction of Günther Behnisch, Frei Otto, Fritz Auer, Jörg Schlaich, Rudolf Bergermann, and others.»
- Datos técnicos: «Roof area 74,000 m²; Cable net mesh width 75 x 75 cm; Edge cables locked coil ropes; Joints and connectors cast steel; Masts steel tubes; Cover acrylic glass (Plexiglas).»
- «resulted in the first iterative computer application for such problems and (of special significance for the "High-Tech Architecture" that followed) in the renaissance of cast steel in architecture.»

## Fuente 10 — Schmid & Weber, «The experiments on measurement models for the Munich Olympic site»
URL: https://api.taylorfrancis.com/content/chapters/oa-edit/download?identifierName=doi&identifierValue=10.1201%2F9781003173359-81 — snippet PDF

- «The designing process for these widespan lightweight structures of unprecedented dimensions was only possible with the help of experiments using physical models and the development of new measurement and calculation methods.»
- «For the Munich construction form-finding models, mainly at a scale of 1:200, women's stockings were used to simulate the shape of the roofing by tensioning and lifting it with wooden sticks (Möller 2015).»
- Modelos de medición (Messmodelle) 1:125: «nets of stainless, spring-steel wires (diameter 0.2 mm) with a mesh size of 24 mm» — corresponde a cada 4º cable real.
- «More than 50,000 working hours were invested in the production and testing of these physical models during the project (Meissner 2016).»
- «Montreal micrometre»: instrumento desarrollado en el pabellón de Montreal 67 (Gutbrod+Otto) para medir tensiones en modelos.
- Inspiración directa: «the lightweight cable net of the German Pavilion for the International Exhibition in Montreal in 1967, constructed by the Stuttgart architects, Rolf Gutbrod and Frei Otto».
- Heinz Isler trabajó la subestructura del estadio.

## Fuente 11 — Hollyday, «Form Finding for Anticlastic Membrane Structures» (bigwalls.net)
URL: https://bigwalls.net/download/membrane.pdf — snippet PDF

- «Form finding is the process of determining the equilibrium shape with a given pre-stress, applied loads, and membrane properties.»
- «Most form finding programs are based on a 3-node triangular grid which is advantageous for subsequent patterning.»
- «There are three main methods used to find the equilibrium shape. All lead to the same result, which is a minimum surface for a given pre stress, membrane characteristics, and edge and support conditions.»
- «The final form the designer chooses may vary from the equilibrium surface so as to be optimized for estimated load extremes and considerations of on-site construction and pre-stressing methods.»

## Fuente 12 — BTES UMass, «Otto's Pneumatic Experiments»
URL: https://openpublishing.library.umass.edu/btes/article/516/galley/466/download/ — snippet PDF

- «Pneumatic membranes (pneus) are containers of space that use differential pressure only for support. They are nearly weightless, they can be easily deployed, and can theoretically span for miles using membranes and cables. The limitations on their capacity are economic, social, and energy-based rather than structural.»
- «From 1957-1960, through funding provided by Stromeyer, he documented a set of experimental pneumatic structures for his book Tensile Structures, Vol. 1 (1962).»
- «Otto focused on pneumatics… because of their resemblance to biological forms and organic life. He argued that structural forms derived from technical logic would become, "…nearer to organic life."»
